Co-founding partners

Meet the two founding businesses.

Both have been operating in Chicagoland for years. Both saw the same problem from different angles. Both put real services on the table — not just logo placement.

CO-FOUNDER · CFW

Chicago Fleet Wraps

Established 2001 · 19,400+ vehicles wrapped · Chicago, IL

A vehicle wrap company that has installed graphics on over 19,400 vehicles since 2001 across Chicagoland. CFW operates a heated, climate-controlled installation bay at 4711 N Lamon Ave in Chicago. Their work spans single-vehicle small-business wraps to 600-vehicle Rivian fleet rollouts for Amazon DSP and Blue Origin.

CFW's reasoning for co-founding the program: businesses they help in their first 90 days often come back two or three years later when they need a full vehicle wrap. The first install is loss-leader marketing; the relationship is the asset.

Contributes to TNBP

Free design, materials, and professional installation of up to 8 sq ft of die-cut cast vinyl graphics on each recipient's business vehicle. All work performed at the CFW Chicago bay. Same materials used on five-thousand-dollar commercial wrap jobs.

CO-FOUNDER · RPM

Regions PM

Property management · Chicagoland

A property management firm with deep visibility into Chicagoland's commercial real estate inventory — office space, retail storefronts, warehouses, and storage. Regions PM understands the difference between what new businesses think they need and what actually works in their first three years of operation.

Regions PM joined as a co-founder because they kept seeing new tenants overcommit to spaces they couldn't grow into — or undercommit to spaces they outgrew in six months. The 1-hour consultation is the same advisory service they provide paying clients, designed to keep founders from making expensive early-stage real estate mistakes.

Contributes to TNBP

Free 1-hour property search consultation including current listings matched to recipient needs, neighborhood-level rent benchmarks, and lease term guidance. Same advisory service provided to paying clients.

regionspm.com →

Why this exists

Most new businesses spend their first 90 days on the basics.

Getting licensed. Opening a business bank account. Finding the first customer. Locking down a place to work from. Marketing comes last. Usually long after the truck has been driving around unmarked for months and the founder has been working from a kitchen table for half a year. The New Business Project covers two of the first essentials — a branded vehicle and a real workspace — so when your business shows up to job number one, it looks legitimate from day one.

Ready to apply?

If your business license is less than 90 days old and you're in Chicagoland, the program is open to you.
